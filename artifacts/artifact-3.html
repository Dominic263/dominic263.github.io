The Kitchen That Learns — A Neural Network Analogy

Introduction: An illustrated explainer that teaches how neural networks work — layers, weights, and backpropagation — through a restaurant kitchen, written for readers with zero technical background.

Description

This artifact began as a class discussion post: I kept trying to explain neural networks with standard node-and-arrow diagrams and they kept falling flat, so I rebuilt the explanation around a restaurant kitchen. The prep cook who chops without knowing the dish is an input-layer neuron; the line of stations passing partial work forward is the network's layers; a connection's weight is how much one cook trusts what the previous cook sends; and the head chef's "too salty" traveling backwards down the line — each station making a small adjustment — is backpropagation.

For the portfolio, I expanded the original post into a designed, illustrated web page. Two hand-coded SVG diagrams carry the explanation: one maps the kitchen line onto an actual network layer diagram, station by station; the other shows the learning loop — the dish flowing forward and the correction flowing backward. The piece closes with the analogy's honest limits (real neurons fire in parallel, not in sequence) and a full AI-use disclosure.

Objective

Created for the third portfolio artifact assignment in AIML-500, which asks for a class activity demonstrating a new skill — and specifically for customizing that work for a portfolio audience. The objective here was twofold: to demonstrate that I genuinely understand how neural networks learn (layers, weights, backpropagation, and why trained networks are black boxes), and to demonstrate that I can translate that understanding for a non-technical audience — the stakeholder-communication skill that AI/ML leadership actually runs on.

Process

  1. Finding the analogy: after standard diagrams fell flat, I searched for a system people already understand where distributed workers each do one small step without seeing the whole — and landed on the restaurant kitchen line.
  2. Stress-testing it: I deliberately probed where the analogy breaks (real neurons work in parallel, not in sequence) so the piece teaches intuition without overselling the metaphor.
  3. Cutting unearned jargon: my first draft leaned on terms like "activation function" that the analogy hadn't earned; I stripped the vocabulary down to only what the kitchen could honestly carry.
  4. Expanding for the portfolio: I turned the discussion post into a designed explainer page and hand-coded two SVG diagrams — the kitchen-to-network mapping and the backpropagation loop — so the visuals teach the same story the words do.

Tools & Technologies Used

  • HTML & CSS (designed explainer page, consistent with this portfolio's system)
  • Hand-coded SVG (both diagrams drawn as code — no diagramming tool)
  • GitHub Pages (public hosting)
  • Claude (Anthropic) — brainstorming and stress-testing the analogy, disclosed in full on the piece itself

Value Proposition

Unique Value

Anyone can recite the definition of backpropagation; explaining it so a non-technical reader feels it is a different skill, and it's one of mine. This artifact shows I understand the mechanics deeply enough to compress them into an everyday system without lying — including knowing exactly where the metaphor stops being true. It's the same translation skill I practiced teaching machine learning to high school students through AR, applied in writing.

Relevance

Every AI initiative lives or dies on whether decision-makers actually understand what the system does. Leaders who can explain AI/ML to boards, clients, and teammates — benefits, limits, and honest uncertainty — are the ones organizations trust with change. This piece is a working sample of that, and a resource any reader can borrow the next time someone asks them how neural networks learn.

Attribution & AI Use

The analogy, writing, and diagrams are my own work. I used Claude (Anthropic) to brainstorm and refine the logic — including asking it to strip unearned jargon and identify where the kitchen analogy breaks down. The full disclosure appears on the explainer itself.
